\begin{keyrule}
\textbf{The one rule: rigor over hype --- and propose, don't impose.} This plan's whole edge is
that Creo is the voice that \emph{won't} overclaim AI in a market drowning in AI claims. So the
presentation has to \emph{model} that discipline. Lead with the honest insight --- \textbf{AI
value is a map, not a multiplier}, and the durable play is a \textbf{swap, not a purge}. Present
the AI direction as our \emph{evidence-based recommendation} (Creo decides --- we're not imposing
it), and meet the credibility question head-on instead of dodging it. \textbf{Never sell AI
harder than the evidence} --- overselling is the exact failure mode the positioning is built to
exploit, so doing it on stage would sink the whole pitch.
\end{keyrule}

\section{The reveal discipline --- the depth ladder}
% ======================================================================
For every core claim, have three levels ready and \emph{stop at the level the question
demands}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{L1 --- the room.} One sentence, no jargon. (Fiona, or Connor opening.)
  \item \textbf{L2 --- the analyst.} The mechanics, in Connor's own coursework --- the ratios,
  the rehire-fraction read, CAC/LTV, and the research lineage. (For Bryan / Akshita / Chelsi.)
  \item \textbf{L3 --- the boundary.} Only the \emph{post-deal measurement} (the Value-Creation
  Bridge) reaches here: one line --- ``that's our proprietary measurement methodology; the applied
  version is self-contained and reconciles to the dollar'' --- nothing to defend; then return to
  the plan.
\end{itemize}

\section{Q\&A playbook}
% ======================================================================
The probes this panel will actually throw, with the answer at the right depth. (\C{} fields the
quant ones; \F{} the positioning ones, as marked.)

\subsection{``Everyone says they do AI --- why believe Creo?'' \textnormal{(Bryan --- the core probe)}}
\textbf{L1:} ``That \emph{is} the position. The market is full of people overclaiming AI; Creo is
the independent, buyer-side voice that won't --- we tell you what AI \emph{can't} durably do.''
\textbf{L2:} ``And we don't dodge our own credibility (next answer). The Diagnostic is buyer-side
\emph{assessment}, not us claiming to build everything --- the neutrality is the scarce asset in a
hype market.''

\subsection{``Does Creo actually have the AI chops to deliver this?'' \textnormal{(Rich / Akshita --- the make-or-break)}}
``Straight answer: the position only works if Creo is demonstrably real on AI, so we're
recommending you \emph{close that gap deliberately} --- orchestrate, don't build: Creo is the
independent diligence-and-architecture brain; specialist vendors do the build. Whether you add a
senior AI hire, partner, or embed one is a live decision --- and we'd vet any hire with Creo's
own `is this AI real?' rigor.'' \emph{(Owning this openly is a strength --- it proves the
rigor-over-hype brand.)}

\subsection{``Isn't this just McKinsey QuantumBlack / BCG X / Bain / the Big Four?'' \textnormal{(Bryan / Chelsi)}}
\textbf{L1:} ``They sell broad transformation and \emph{the build} --- which makes their read
conflicted. No one owns the independent, buyer-side, diligence-grade seat.'' \textbf{L2:} ``Bain
works with $\sim$80\% of the largest sponsors; BCG owns `reshape.' We don't out-scale them --- we
\emph{flank} them on independence, deal-level rigor, and the reselection read they don't do. A
boutique wins on focus and distribution, not size.''

\subsection{``Is the AI roll-up real, or just hype?'' \textnormal{(Rich)}}
``Real and funded --- Elad Gil's thesis, General Catalyst (Long Lake took Amex GBT private for
\$6.3B), Thrive Holdings --- across legal, accounting, IT, BPO, healthcare. \emph{But} the
returns aren't there yet: BCG finds only $\sim$5\% capture real value while 60\% lag, and
$\sim$95\% of GenAI pilots fail. That gap --- hot strategy, no rigorous read --- is precisely the
opening.''

\subsection{``1000:1 --- won't AI just replace these workers?'' \textnormal{(anyone)}}
``No --- that's the hype the plan corrects. The handoff is a \emph{map}: $\sim$3:1 in knowledge
work, $\sim$10{,}000:1 only in pure coordination (the Uber dispatch layer), $\sim$1:1 in
judgment, physical, relationship, and regulated work. Price the mix, not the average.''

\subsection{``Your `reselection' idea --- isn't that just layoffs?'' \textnormal{(Akshita)}}
``It's a \emph{swap, not a purge}. You keep and amplify the people who truly understand the
business, collapse the busywork, and re-select for AI-fluent talent --- without breaking quality.
Gut-and-replace is the failure mode: Klarna went AI-only on support and had to re-hire humans.''

\subsection{``Where's the Microsoft data from?'' \textnormal{(Bryan)}}
``Public record: Microsoft cut 15{,}000+ in 2025, made AI use `no longer optional' and tied it to
performance reviews (the Liuson memo), and shielded its AI/Copilot teams from the 2026 freeze ---
all reported and sourced in the plan. We use only the documented version.'' \emph{(Stay on the
sourced facts --- don't speculate past the public record.)}

\subsection{``What would a sponsor actually pay for?'' \textnormal{(Rich)}}
\F/\C: ``An independent read that \emph{reprices the deal} --- tells them what the target is
really worth once you strip the hype ratio. Entry is a free mini-Diagnostic; it ladders to a
productized Diagnostic, an amplification engagement, and a recurring portfolio read.''

\subsection{``Can a boutique really compete here?'' \textnormal{(Rich / strategy)}}
``Not on scale --- on focus, independence, credibility, and \emph{distribution}: your warm
sponsor relationships are an edge the big firms can't buy. Being first to own the independent
`what can durably be automated, and can this business execute the swap' seat is the moat.''

\subsection{``This seems obvious.'' \textnormal{(Bryan / Chelsi --- name it first)}}
\textbf{Pre-empt it; say it before they do.} ``This is going to feel obvious the moment we draw
the map --- and that's the point; the best frameworks do. The opportunity is that \emph{nobody in
your market actually does it} --- they're all still pricing AI at one hype multiple. Obvious-but-
nobody-does-it is the most ownable position there is.'' \emph{Welcome the reaction --- it means
they believe it. It's the good reaction, not a threat.}

\subsection{``How do you prove the value was actually created, post-deal?'' \textnormal{(Akshita / Rich)}}
``We close the loop with independent measurement --- the Value-Creation Bridge --- separating
real operational gains from value merely transferred. Diligence $\rightarrow$ amplification
$\rightarrow$ \emph{measured} result.'' \textbf{If pushed past the applied method:} the hard-stop
line below --- then back to the plan.

\section{The hard stop (post-deal measurement)}
% ======================================================================
The \emph{only} place a deflection is needed is the post-deal measurement (the Bridge). Use
verbatim, if pushed past the applied method:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The hard stop:} ``That's our proprietary measurement methodology --- the applied
  method here is self-contained and, as you can see, reconciles to the dollar.'' Then return to
  the plan.
\end{itemize}
Keep it to that line and move on. Do \textbf{not} gesture at any larger body of theory, and do
\textbf{not} reference formal proofs or verification of any kind --- you won't be able to field
the follow-up, and you don't need to.

\section{Landmines --- do not}
% ======================================================================
\begin{landmine}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Don't oversell Creo's current AI capability.} Be honest about the gap and the
  build/partner plan --- overclaiming is the exact thing the brand critiques.
  \item \textbf{Don't claim 1000:1 / wholesale replacement.} It's a map and amplification; the
  ratios are illustrative orders of magnitude, not constants.
  \item \textbf{Stick to the public record on Microsoft} --- only the documented facts (the cuts,
  the ``AI is no longer optional'' memo, the AI teams shielded from the freeze). Don't speculate
  past what's sourced.
  \item \textbf{Don't promise to build the AI systems.} Orchestrate, don't build --- Creo is the
  independent brain; vendors build.
  \item \textbf{Don't impose the AI direction.} It's our \emph{recommendation}; lead by
  understanding what management wants, and adapt if they want something else.
  \item \textbf{Don't invoke ``formally verified,'' a ``proof,'' or any larger framework} behind
  the measurement. Keep it to the proprietary-methodology line and return to the plan.
  \item \textbf{Don't assert self-reported market figures as fact} (Bain's ``80\%,'' vendor
  allocations) --- attribute them.
\end{itemize}
\end{landmine}
